# Visual Research Contract

The purpose of research is to make a product-specific design decision visible. It is not a mood-board
collection exercise and it is not proof that the final prototype works.

## Source Routing

Use the first credible source:

1. Preserve explicit operator-provided brand rules, screenshots, Figma frames, or an approved design
   system as the primary constraint.
2. If the `refero-design` skill is actually available, use it for design research while this onsite
   skill remains authoritative for product scope, outputs, confirmation, privacy, and stopping.
3. Otherwise, if Refero MCP capabilities are available, use them directly.
4. Otherwise, use other authorized visible references.
5. If no external source is available, create two visibly different Agent-derived annotated
   compositions and label them as fallback evidence.

Do not ask the operator to install Refero merely to continue. Availability is a runtime fact, not a
memory from a previous task.

## Refero Method

When using Refero:

- search **Styles** to establish visual language and overall impression;
- inspect **Screens** for information hierarchy, concrete business states, density, media role, and
  component behavior;
- inspect **Flows** when a multi-step journey or state transition is central to the proposition;
- search using sanitized product form, industry, desired impression, and interaction terms;
- show or link the actual selected reference image, screen, or flow whenever the tool permits it;
- lock one primary foundation; give secondary references one narrow named responsibility.

Common MCP capabilities may include `refero_search_styles`, `refero_get_style`,
`refero_search_screens`, `refero_get_screen`, `refero_get_screen_image`, `refero_search_flows`, and
`refero_get_flow`. Do not assume exact tool names or availability; inspect the current runtime.

Never copy a product wholesale, average unrelated references into a safe midpoint, claim third-party
imagery as customer evidence, or reuse protected assets without authorization.

## Internal Alternatives

Explore at least two credible routes per card internally. They must differ in experience topology or
composition and in at least one of attention order, interaction feedback, media role, information
hierarchy, or state choreography.

Do not count as separate routes:

- the same dashboard with another color palette;
- different fonts, radius, shadows, or border strength;
- moving identical cards into another grid;
- adding decorative gradients, glass effects, or generic AI sparkles;
- putting the same loop behind a chat shell without changing how the business state is understood.

Record only a compact rejection reason for unselected alternatives. The human board shows one
recommended route per product, not every internal experiment.

## Reference Lock

For every selected route record:

- intended impression and why it fits this product and customer moment;
- exact primary reference ID or URL, or `Agent-derived fallback`;
- any secondary reference and its narrow role;
- target first-screen composition and attention order;
- entry, processing or decision, result, and reset states;
- information hierarchy, density, typography character, semantic color roles, component character,
  and imagery or media role;
- one distinguishing visual or interaction move;
- `reference trait -> business reason -> target screen/state consequence` mappings;
- explicit rejects;
- the reskin counterfactual.

The reskin counterfactual answers: after ignoring palette, typography, radius, border, and shadow,
what remains recognizably designed for this business moment? If there is no concrete answer, the
route is a reskin and cannot be selected.

## Portfolio Distinctness

Compare all selected routes together. Identify repeated shells, dominant colors, navigation models,
hero layouts, card grids, table treatments, media strategies, and state feedback. Repetition is
allowed only when an approved shared design system or customer brand requires it and the product
mechanisms remain distinct through interaction and composition.

Do not force arbitrary difference. The objective is a portfolio of credible experiences, not a
style sampler. Every difference must help the boss understand a different product idea.

## Visible Preview

Each selected route needs at least one visible target composition referenced from the board and
Design Card. It may be:

- an authorized reference-backed annotated screen map;
- an SVG or raster composition;
- a lightweight HTML design preview without business logic;
- another directly viewable artifact supported by the current environment.

The preview must show the primary action, attention order, core business object, and either the
processing/decision or result state. Use additional state frames only when needed to explain the
interaction. Label it as design evidence, not a working prototype.
